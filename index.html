Four things that never go in the kettle

  • No syrup shortcuts

    Grain gets milled here at six in the morning and mashed by hand. The spent grain goes to a pig farm up the Allegheny, and the pigs have opinions.

  • No rushed lagers

    Our lagers sit near freezing for six weeks. A bigger brewery would call that wasted tank space. We call it the reason they taste this clean.

  • No stale hops

    Hops arrive vacuum-sealed and get used inside the month. Anything older goes home with the staff, who are not complaining.

  • No filtered-to-death

    We filter enough to be bright, never enough to be boring. If it tastes like a press release, it doesn't leave the tank.

From grain to glass

  1. 01

    Mill and mash

    Four hundred pounds of grain cracked at 6 am and stirred into hot water by someone holding a paddle and a coffee.

  2. 02

    The loud boil

    Ninety minutes at a rolling boil. The kettle rattles its lid, hops go in three times, and the street smells like toast and pine.

  3. 03

    Slow ferment

    Yeast does the real work for two weeks in steel tanks named after retired tram drivers. We check the temperature and say thanks.

  4. 04

    Cold rest

    Everything rests cold until it's bright and calm. Lagers get six weeks of it; the hazy stuff gets three days and a pep talk.

  5. 05

    Tank to tap

    Bright tanks feed the taps through forty feet of chilled line. Nothing is bottled, trucked or warmed up before it hits your glass.

The tram shed

Forty feet of bar, sixteen taps and long tables built from the depot's old floorboards. Bring friends, bring the dog, bring an appetite for whatever truck parked outside tonight.

  • Open seven days

    From 3 pm on weekdays and noon at weekends. Last call is called loudly, so nobody misses it.

  • Dogs welcome

    Water bowls at the door, biscuits behind the bar. Cats are also welcome, but they never come.

  • Food truck nightly

    Tacos Tuesday to Thursday, smash burgers Friday to Sunday. You can also bring your own.

Ask the bar

Can I bring my dog?

Yes, inside and on the patio, as long as they're leashed and friendly. Water bowls are by the door and the biscuits live behind the bar.

Are kids allowed?

Until 8 pm, with a grown-up. We keep house root beer on tap and crayons at the host stand.

Do you serve food?

A food truck parks outside every night we're open. You're also welcome to bring your own food or have it delivered to the bar.

Can I take beer home?

Everything on the board goes into 32 oz crowlers, sealed at the bar. Bring a clean growler and we'll fill that too.

Anything gluten-free?

Our beers aren't, but we always pour a gluten-reduced pale and two dry ciders from an orchard friend in Butler County.

Can I book the whole place?

The back room seats forty and the whole shed a hundred and sixty. Send the form at the bottom of the page and we'll call you within a day.
